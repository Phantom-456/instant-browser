Privacy policy

Talaria (QuickPeek). Last updated 17 November 2026.

Talaria is a small app for your computer. It opens small browser windows from a hotkey. This page says what it does with your data. The short version: it has no server of its own, it has no analytics, and it sends nothing about you to the people who make it.

What stays on your computer

  • Your settings, your pins (their addresses, names and colours), and the saved pictures of released pages are saved in the app's folder on your computer.
  • Web pages you open are loaded straight from the sites you visit, like in any browser. Their cookies and site data stay on your computer, in the app's own storage.
  • If you turn it on, the search bar reads the history files of the browsers on your computer to suggest your recent searches and pages. That history stays in memory on your computer and is never sent anywhere. Cookies and passwords of other browsers are never read.

What the app sends over the internet

  • The web pages you open, to the sites themselves.
  • What you type in the search bar, to the search engine you chose (for example Google or DuckDuckGo), only if you leave search suggestions on, so it can suggest searches. Turn suggestions off in Settings to send nothing until you press Enter.
  • A request for updates, to GitHub, to find out if a new version exists.
  • The small icon of a page (the favicon), from the site you pinned, to show on its pin.

Google Drive sync (optional, off until you connect it)

If you choose to connect Google Drive, Talaria asks Google for one permission only: https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata. It lets the app keep one small file in a hidden folder of your own Google Drive that only this app can see. It does not let the app see, read, change or delete any of your other files in Drive.

What goes into that file:

  • your pins: address, title, the name you gave, colours, and whether it is archived;
  • your bookmarks;
  • a short list of preferences that make sense on another computer;
  • only if you turn each one on in Settings: the addresses you opened from this app (not the history of your other browsers), and the pages you have open on each computer, with the computer's name.

What never goes into it: passwords, cookies, logged-in sessions, site storage, the pictures of released pages, where windows and pins sit on your screen, and the history of your other browsers.

The file is locked on your computer before it is uploaded. You choose a passphrase. The app turns it into a key and locks the file with AES-256-GCM, so Google, and anyone who gets into your Google account, only ever holds scrambled data. The passphrase and the key never leave your computers (the key is kept in the system keychain). If you forget the passphrase and have no other unlocked computer, nobody, including the people who make Talaria, can recover the synced data. You can delete the file and start again. The file is stored in your Google Drive and nowhere else. The people who make Talaria cannot see it and have no server that receives it.

You can stop at any time. Disconnecting in the app removes the connection, and the app can delete the synced file. You can also remove the permission yourself at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Talaria's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The data is used only to give you the sync feature. It is not sold, not used for advertising, and not shared with anyone.

Children

Talaria is not made for children and does not knowingly collect any information from them.
